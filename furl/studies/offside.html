A rail for things you might use again

An ordered, scrollable offside space. Rows and expressions share the rail; each parked piece is inert syntax until it returns to the program.

This standalone sketch uses a small tree of preset syntax templates, not Hazel editors, evaluation or typechecking. The earlier version only replaced entire expression fields. This revision lets you fill nested holes and replace subterms; arbitrary text editing is outside this sketch.

Park, scroll, and bring back

Choose Move or Copy. Drag a row from its name, or an expression from a name, literal, operator or delimiter. Drop near the rail or between its cards; a drop above or below the collection joins that end. Scroll the rail with your trackpad. Its corner button tucks it away and brings it back.

Try Copy: put □ + □ into area, then drop □ * □ into one of its holes. Click-to-pick and click-to-place also work; Escape cancels.

Parked cards have no internal edit targets. Move takes a card out; Copy leaves it on the rail. A row returns as a row; an expression fills a hole or replaces the term whose handle you target. One completed action is one Undo. Tab and Space/Enter pick up or place; focus the rail and use ↑/↓ to choose an insertion slot. The rail scrolls through the pieces present, with room at both ends; it is not an infinite canvas.

A small starter collection

The starter cards use the same Move and Copy controls as everything you park. There is no separate palette yet: use Copy to reuse a starter, or Move to consume it. Reset restores the collection.

Syntax on the rail, meaning at the destination

Parked pieces keep their spelling and structure, with no captured runtime environment or promise of their former binding. A free name is resolved again where the piece returns. For example, a parked x + 1 can use the destination’s x. This is useful scrap syntax, outside evaluation.

The earlier metadata proposal would remember which binding each free name referred to, making a “preserve meaning” policy easier to check and explaining refused drops under shadowing. That is useful for a refactoring clipboard, but unnecessary for this first rail. In live Furl, parking still removes source, and reinsertion still goes through Hazel’s scope/type checks and the selected policy; storage alone does not make either operation a refactor.

Rows and terms remain separate cards; no joining, staggering or editable mini-programs yet. The shelf and patches arrangements are retired. The rail’s view state—scroll and collapse—is separate from source edits and Undo.
